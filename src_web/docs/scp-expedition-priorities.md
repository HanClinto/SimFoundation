# SCP Expedition Priorities

Draft for review, 2026-09-09. Companion to the [59-work proposal catalog](scp-expedition-proposals.md), which contains the source links, individual missions, scores, caveats and additional candidates. This is a recommended content portfolio and dependency order, not an approved implementation schedule.

**Player direction, 2026-09-10 (#40):** prioritize a grounded
observation/research -> discovery -> physical engineering -> useful equipment
or procedure -> new field/containment decision loop. Research progression and
bounded crafting are desired, not rejected by earlier portfolio limits.
Transport is now reusable without lifetime tickets; finite supplies and actual
travel remain. Choose additional sources for this gameplay payoff, not merely
more maintenance chores or another collectible name. Inventory redesign and
fog-of-war remain deferred. The controlled kinetic study now enables physical
restraint engineering; actual recovered impact records enable a protective
tradeoff after loss. The subsequently added SCP-173 annex uses direct watch and
three-person supervised work after prior research. This second containment
investment is distinct from the already proven SCP-2006 curation loop, not a
generic psychology framework or an assumption that passive visibility is watch.

### Approved Dangerous-Capability Continuation

The peaceful/source-backed bounds above are not product exclusions. The
original [intervention and containment loop](../src/simulation/catalog/campaign/README.md#equipment-backed-intervention)
now adds actual worn tools/armor, finite subdual, physical restraints with
struggle and band exchange, prepared holding with upkeep/warnings, finite
emergency lockdown, breach/recontainment, and safe secured medical transfer.
Opted-in mortality retains permanent bodies/cargo. Recovery uses surviving
members of the ordinary starting roster; the dedicated reserve pair and its
dispatch mechanism have been removed. Total roster loss leaves no replacements.

Surviving staff have separate supply-backed wound and blood courses. Actual
equipment can be repaired at a bench and rearmed from finite supplies, retaining
item identity and spent-condition/charge consequences. The
[connected danger walkthrough](../src/simulation/catalog/campaign/tests/connected-danger.txt)
incurs actual injury, recovers the living hostile, cares for staff and maintains
containment through a real assigned worker. Subjective feedback issue #40
remains valuable but does not block these already approved capabilities.

## Recommendation

Build a small set of complete expedition-to-facility stories before increasing the anomaly count. Start with **SCP-1867, SCP-1370 and a bounded SCP-294**. Follow with **SCP-507 and SCP-2295** once living recovery and medical intake are real, then use **SCP-1295** to bring facility-style work into the field. Reserve **SCP-173 and SCP-3008** as showcase milestones, not the next two enemy types.

The central missing loop is:

**An opportunity appears -> staff prepare physical equipment -> field decisions produce people, objects or evidence -> return requires appropriate intake -> physical study/care changes future work or opens a corroborated lead.**

Most of the cost is in making that loop work, not drawing more SCPs. Current expeditions already preserve team identity, supplies, injury and carried objects. They do not yet admit civilian/resident passengers, complete knowledge-only or service objectives, preserve visited sites, or run the base's work systems on field maps. Existing material vessels are not living transport, and existing World/Recorded rendering is not a cognition system.

Two useful substitutions:

- **SCP-348 instead of SCP-2295** when medical anatomy is too expensive. It offers a modest, personal care routine, not the same mechanical benefit, at F3/E2.
- **SCP-2006 instead of SCP-173** when behavioral containment is more interesting than watch coverage. A few authored interactions and curated screenings offer F5/E3 without implementing arbitrary shapeshifting.

SCP-1437 is the best subsequent recurring intake site. Add it after persistent revisits and sample handling, not as an infinite randomized loot source. SCP-529 and SCP-999 support missions are inexpensive variety once resident care has actual supplies and ownership.

## Feature Order

### 1. Outcomes, Evidence And Safe Return

**Priority: foundational; partly anticipated by the roadmap, not implemented.**

Extend the current all-cargo completion rule with a small typed set: recovered object identity, recorded observation, escorted person, serviced target, and deliberate withdrawal. Implement only the first two initially. Store dated evidence with its source and the operation that produced it. Add a single actual-condition-triggered notice after one successful study; no random world-event engine yet.

Define return outcomes before adding more danger. The current all-team recovery requirement can softlock when nobody can provide aid. Decide a bounded rescue/assisted withdrawal policy that preserves consequences and costs. Do not hide free healing behind recall, silently lose a pawn, or implement permanent death without a separate design decision.

**First proof:** SCP-1867 collection plus existing Service Store 3. Returning evidence without every optional object can complete the main objective; partial return/reload cannot duplicate cargo, findings or leads. A return report explains what remains unresolved. Keep optional evidence separate from required cargo rather than changing all scenarios to permissive success.

**Unlocks:** essentially the whole catalog, especially SCP-1171, SCP-179, SCP-701 and the investigative tales. This should be a small extension to authored scenario definitions and shared execution, not a quest language.

### 2. Physical Study And Bounded Object Use

**Priority: foundational; approved direction, currently absent as a general work loop.**

Require a reachable installed workbench or relevant device, an available worker, the identified specimen/record, required supplies and elapsed simulation work. Produce a dated observation and retain sample/output identity. Start with one corroboration task and one small device interaction. Do not add research points, a tech tree or instant discovery commands.

**First proof:** The SCP-1867 journal and a specimen support one specific lead; an unrelated specimen does not. Next, SCP-294 dispenses from an authored request set and its traceable mixture test subtracts the same amount from the source. Cancellation retains already consumed inputs and existing outputs correctly.

**Unlocks:** SCP-1370, SCP-294, SCP-348, SCP-1981, SCP-1437, evidence-focused SCP-3001 and many later apparatus. It makes the returned content useful instead of merely displayable.

### 3. Field Handling And Living Intake

**Priority: foundational for capture/rescue; roadmap-aligned but substantial new work.**

First bring actual cases and tools into a field manifest, and reuse base vessel/handling rules through the field command boundary. A field seal action must take work and appropriate equipment; the worn case remains worn after arrival. Separate bulky freight from hand-carried cargo.

Then add cooperative field people/residents with stable identity, follow/wait, interruption, transport membership, a valid destination and care needs. Keep consent and restraint distinct. Implement escort before hostile capture. Add casualty carry/assisted evacuation with explicit carrier ownership and equipment costs before relying on incapacitated civilians.

**First proof:** The proposed original damaged-specimen courier conserves the specimen/source and case condition across transfer. The original care-transfer scenario, then SCP-507, preserves one living identity through escort, interrupted movement, transit and base or external admission. A refused or blocked arrival reports a reason; no one becomes an archive-case object.

**Unlocks:** SCP-507, SCP-529, SCP-1545, SCP-2295, later SCP-049/SCP-191/SCP-2662. SCP-1370 needs only the early mobile-object handling subset, not the whole person system.

### 4. Small Medical And Care Extensions

**Priority: high for lasting expedition consequences; medicine exists, these treatment semantics do not.**

For SCP-2295, add one explicit organ-trauma state and corresponding replacement record, usable nearby textiles, a treatment ownership interval, and ordinary continued care. Its patient-selection rule must be visible; unrelated injuries remain. Do not assume body-region labels already constitute an organ model.

**First proof:** Two eligible patients in range demonstrate the source-based age preference; supplying fabric prevents the modeled self-material cost. Interrupted treatment, current-version save/reload and return maintain the correct patient and remaining supplies. Brain injury is not repaired by a generic fallback.

**Unlocks:** SCP-2295 directly, better ordinary rescue and later specialized patients. Broader surgery, pediatric population simulation and universal anomaly cures remain outside scope.

### 5. Field Work And Persistent Revisit State

**Priority: high; reuse of existing systems requires explicit new integration.**

Enable only the local work required by the first mission: delivery, one repair, meal access and service staffing. Scope orders, reservations, supplies and clocks to the field world. Do not duplicate the entire base state as a nested game. Preserve enough off-site state for a visit to matter, including removed cargo, completed repairs, contact agreements and pending service needs.

**First proof:** SCP-1295 remains accessible because actual food delivery and repair work finish. Returning and revisiting does not replenish taken supplies or reset the service problem. A missed opportunity produces an explained, bounded consequence rather than an unannounced campaign loss. Only one active expedition remains supported.

**Unlocks:** SCP-1295, SCP-1437, SCP-2952, ground support for SCP-179, and eventually SCP-3008. Do not implement a strategic map, vehicle fuel economy or recurring obligations for dozens of sites at once.

### 6. Choose One Containment Showcase

**Priority: after the return loop works; capability depends on the selected showcase.**

For **SCP-173**, build actor attention, sustained watch, observer handoff, competing work and a small cleaning task. Cameras/LOS are not equivalent to conscious direct observation. Test whether players can design robust staffing coverage instead of performing repetitive micro-management. Make relevant states openly inspectable.

For **SCP-2006**, build bounded dialogue/acting actions, curated media access and a small belief-response state. Test whether scheduling and curation create distinct choices without turning into endless required conversations. No language model or unbounded shapeshifter is necessary.

These are different investments. Do not bundle both into a generic psychological containment framework. Prove one, then decide whether its reusable parts justify the other.

### 7. Build The Bounded SCP-3008 Showcase

**Priority: later integration, not an early prototype with disconnected shortcuts.**

Use the already-working escort, casualty handling, field work and timed outcomes. Add one authored sector, a few threat actors, a legible closing-time behavior change and an explicitly adapted extraction window. A normal player should be able to deliver supplies, repair the shelter, gather a group and choose when to leave.

**First proof:** Evacuees, casualties, finite cargo and the original team survive the same return accounting. No duplication after partial success. A blocked exit has an explicit recovery path. Base staff continue ordinary work while the team is away. Assess decisions and consequences rather than exact completion ticks.

Only expand to changing topology or another sector after this version is fun. Its best contribution is combining systems, not becoming an independent infinite-store survival game inside the application.

## Review And Stop Gates

After the first three candidates, pause content expansion and check these hypotheses in play:

1. Does each expedition demand a choice beyond walking to every marker? Remove optional objectives that only lengthen the route.
2. Does a recovered item or observation change a later action at the base? Cut rewards that only populate an inert list.
3. Can the player understand why handling, study, care or return is blocked without reading the source wiki?
4. Can a failed or partial operation continue into a coherent campaign, with people/resources accounted for and no exploit through retry or reload?
5. Can ordinary containment upkeep be scheduled robustly, without mandatory repetitive clicks?
6. Does the second scenario reuse working behavior from the first? If not, narrow the supposed shared subsystem before expanding it.

The best next implementation is therefore **one evidence-producing physical recovery plus one real study task that yields a specific lead**, with an explicit return/failure contract. It establishes why the player should care about the rest of the catalog.
