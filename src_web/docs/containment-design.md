# Containment as Experimental Engineering

Status: approved direction; not a claim of implemented systems.

## Authored Before Generated

Develop a varied catalog of hand-authored, gameplay-interesting anomalies before attempting procedural generation. Anomalies may be original inventions rather than wiki adaptations. Use provisional internal designations such as `AN-001`; source adaptations retain their article references and separate adaptation notes. Discover what produces interesting decisions before generalizing a generation system.

## Multiple Viable Solutions

Containment depends on interactions among entity behavior, materials, geometry, environmental conditions, sensing, and maintenance. Materials should have different resistance and permeability properties, not a single universal strength tier. A costly structural barrier may still conduct an unwanted signal, admit a harmful substance, or require maintenance a cheaper alternative avoids.

Begin with a small set of inspectable tile attributes and a few authored interactions. Avoid building a general circuitry language before concrete scenarios demonstrate the need. Preserve room for alternative solutions: layered barriers, separation, environmental regulation, controlled access, or behavioral accommodation. Equipment and maintenance should be physically supplied and performed by personnel.

## Knowledge Through Consequences

General impressions and observable actions are common; precise, causal knowledge takes time, instrumentation, examinations, or experiments. An entity may demonstrate a previously unknown property during containment failure. Players may deliberately accept higher risk for faster information, including controlled trials or intentionally weakened containment. Safer observation and testing remain viable; neither catastrophe nor harm to personnel should be the only research mechanism.

Riskier experiments expose actual simulation consequences, not guaranteed knowledge rewards purchased with an abstract loss counter. Evidence must support the conclusion. Discoveries update an evolving record with dated observations, confidence, superseded assumptions, and revised procedures. An observed concrete failure may justify "concrete degradation observed" before its mechanism is confirmed. Institutional understatement and corrective annotations carry the humor; do not turn every finding into a joke or redact operationally necessary information.

## Robust Routines

Staffing coverage, tolerances, redundant barriers, and inspection intervals should accommodate human variability. One missed check should not normally force catastrophe. Failures can accumulate or interact, giving a prepared facility time to detect, isolate, respond, and recover. Emotional, psychotic, and physical breakdowns differ from ordinary preferences or deliberate refusals.

The UI exposes coverage, recorded checks, warning signs, and known exceptions. Hidden causes remain hidden until supported by evidence. Reserved or masking personnel can display a neutral or pleasant outward demeanor despite significant underlying distress; generic moodlets and action/conversation cues are observations, not infallible health meters. Cameras and personnel observations determine whether cues are currently visible or merely remembered.

## Implementation Order

1. Improve the personnel, equipment, medical, and assessment interfaces.
2. Establish explicit facility observations, remembered state, and camera coverage.
3. Introduce autonomous schedules and needs-driven routines with inspectable exceptions.
4. Prove a small material-dependent containment scenario with an original authored anomaly.
5. Expand the authored catalog and only then evaluate generation or richer machinery.

Tactical response is pause-based real-time, integrated with the same facility and personnel rules. Recontainment can require engineering and specialist procedures, not only combat capability.
