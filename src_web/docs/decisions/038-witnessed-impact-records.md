# Witnessed impact records, not disaster XP

The progression loop in #40 should retain useful knowledge after a costly
incident without rewarding omniscient damage history.

An explicit `Observe` commitment operates a carried physical recorder from the
worker's current visible vantage. It neither approaches a threat nor starts
recording at command acceptance. The normal pawn turn activates the watch.
At an actual impact, a bounded side effect checks the active, unblocked watch,
current control/capability/custody, intact recorder ownership and sight of both
attacker and victim. It does not execute a second pawn turn or run a scheduler.

Record after armor calculation, before injury is applied. Thus an already active
operator can capture the impact that incapacitates them. Actual fully absorbed
impacts still have records; zero/capped-away attempts do not. Each commitment
captures once. The queue finds its own receipt and completes even if it filled
the device. Cancellation, unavailable workers or lost visibility do not harvest
earlier events. A permission-paused watch must resume on its own turn.

Records live only on the actual item and name the observer, attacker/definition,
victim, date, modeled incoming/applied severity and worn armor. Their IDs combine
recorder and global tick; a recorder watches one attacker and each actor makes at
most one impact per tick. Capacity is explicit and full devices retain their
records rather than silently evicting evidence. Ordinary transfer/body recovery
preserves the same device and data.

Physical Study can require recorded impacts from a named definition. It selects
qualifying intact sources during work and records the incident IDs in its
finding. Craft already retains that finding's provenance; no parallel knowledge
or rewards ledger is needed.

The original campaign's survey kit holds four records. Recovered kinetic traces
enable a two-pack short-burst vest: reduction20/wear40 rather than the original
10/20. This is a tradeoff, not immunity, resurrection or free equipment repair.
The ordinary-command demonstration loses its observer, recovers the kit/tool,
analyzes and builds, then returns the same threat under improved protection.
The old body and broken vest remain; a second actual clinician stabilizes the
surviving medic. Global inspection remains unchanged: this is a physical
recording capability, not the deferred fog-of-war/camera-network system.
